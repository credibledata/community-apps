About these numbers

Source. The Colorado Avalanche Information Center's public observation API (api.avalanche.state.co.us), downloaded 2 Oct 2026: 29,010 observations reporting 45,536 avalanches. Observations come from forecasters, professionals and the public.

Avalanches vs observations. One observation can report many avalanches (up to 100). Totals count avalanches; a missing count counts as one. Size, aspect and trigger apply to the whole observation.

Seasons. A season is a water year: "2023-24" runs 1 Oct 2023 – 30 Sep 2024. The page starts at 2010-11, when CAIC's digital record becomes consistent; earlier seasons hold only a few back-entered events.

Human-triggered share. Of avalanches with a known trigger, the share set off by skiers, riders, snowshoers, snowmobilers, hikers and other people travelling — not explosives control work. Unknown triggers are excluded from the denominator.

People involved. CAIC's incident flag on the observation: a person or property was caught or involved. The Accidents section has the counts of people caught, buried and killed.

Accidents. CAIC's Colorado accident and incident reports, 1,326 from 1951 to November 2026, merged where a report appears in both records. Deaths per season match CAIC's published fatality table. Names and narrative text are left out; each row links to CAIC's report. About one report in six records the day's danger rating.

Snow and weather. NRCS SNOTEL daily snow water equivalent, snow depth and temperature for 115 Colorado stations, averaged over the stations inside each CAIC zone. Zones have from 4 stations (Gunnison) to 28 (Front Range). New SWE is the day's rise in snow water equivalent.

Ski areas. Ski areas rarely send CAIC their control results, so in-bounds avalanches are almost absent: about 1% of avalanches since 2010-11 are tagged operational.

Reporting bias. More observers means more recorded avalanches. Compare shares and timing across zones with more confidence than raw counts.
